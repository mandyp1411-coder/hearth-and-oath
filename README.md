# Hearth & Oath

Version 0.70.0 is The archive: every save says which rules it lives by, the old rules are kept at their own address, and the measuring tools arrive. No rule changes; nothing is removed.

- **Two games, named.** The codebase has carried two rulebooks since v0.60: lives begun since then (a debt ledger from v0.61, a rolled station from v0.62) live by the new rules, and lives from before, or migrated from before, keep the rules they were written under. From this version every save says so in plain words: a slot, the title card, an export and an import review read **Old rules**, **New rules, begun before v0.70**, or **New rules, begun in v0.70**. A life begun before this version is never given a date after the fact. The exported file is named for its rules (`…-old-rules.json`, `…-new-rules.json`).
- **The Old Rules archive.** The v0.69 game — the last build that carries both rulebooks — is kept as its own build with its own storage, so the two can never share a browser database: IndexedDB `hearth-and-oath-legacy`, every key under `hearth-and-oath-legacy:`, the offline file `hearth-and-oath-old-rules.html`. `node scripts/build-archive.mjs` packages it into `out-legacy/` from the `v0.69-archive` branch; the owner publishes that directory at an address of their choosing and names it in `dist/js/archive-link.js`. The archive labels its slots the same way, names its exports `…-archive.json`, and turns a new-rules import away in a line: it belongs in the main game.
- **An old-rules save here** still plays — nothing is removed in this version — and its slot says where it is going: the label, a line in the game's voice, **Export for the archive**, and **Open the Old Rules archive** once the address is set. The slot is left readable until the player moves it.
- **New-rules saves are carried forward.** Each save now carries `edition.step`, the migration step it has been brought through (70 in this version); each later release that changes what a new-rules save holds adds a step, with the default it gives a life that predates the system stated in that release's notes. Stated now, for the systems that already exist — a new-rules life begun before a system arrived does not get it after the fact: before v0.64 it has no schooling record and reads and writes; before v0.65 no living world, and the regional world keeps its own calendar; before v0.66 no guild record; before v0.67 no delve record, and it keeps the maze; before v0.68 no battlecraft record, and it fights on the older battle page; before v0.69 no faith record, and the congregation is what it was. None is invented after the fact, and none of this changes in this version.
- **Real saves as fixtures.** `tests/fixtures/saves/` holds a life each from the v0.60, v0.62, v0.66 and v0.69 engines (birth, eighteen, a season on), written by `scripts/fixtures/generate-release-saves.mjs` with each release's own code; `tests/edition-v70.test.js` loads, labels and plays them. A v0.60 life has neither marker and lives by the old rules.
- **The measuring tools**, which measure and do not yet judge: the interface harness (`scripts/interface-harness.py`, Playwright at 1440×900 and 390×844, default and largest text — the distance to the decision, the costs on the choices, the Fight page's height, the phone's content share, sub-tab rows, the smallest type, the requests before the title settles, a toast over a control); the voice lint (`scripts/voice-lint.mjs`, every string the player sees against the one-voice rule — 614 hits at this version, the baseline); the content schema and validator (`scripts/content/validate.mjs`, `docs/content-schema.md` — scenes, threads, plays, ambitions, landmarks, places, foes and dialogue as data files, with roles that exist, conditions that parse and voice that passes). Their numbers for this version are committed under `docs/measures/`. None fails the build yet.

Version 0.69.0 is The cloth: the church as a path, with the whole anatomy. New lives only.

- **Ways in.** Given as a child: the keeper asks a poor house for its shrine pupil, once, at nine or ten, and an oblate sweeps and learns the words until the house asks at sixteen whether they will stay. Called: at sixteen, from the congregation, with devotion in you or a name the Order knows, the keeper gives you the novice's grey. Ordained: the Seminary's holy orders, read aloud at a shrine, make a brother or sister without the noviciate. Vowed: when it is bad — a death, a wound — the congregation's people sometimes promise the house the rest of their life, and in the morning the shrine is still there.
- **Daily life.** The offices, three days a season at a shrine; a novice who misses two is sent home. Four seasons kept and the house's regard earned, and the vows: poverty (the house's roof over you and your rent gone, and your surplus above two hundred gold half into the poor-box each year), chastity (courtship shut — the open wing reads it kindly for a brother who already has a household; the strict wing takes no married brothers), obedience (the prior's work each spring: the sick at the lower houses, the burials nobody could pay for, the lamp carried to the crossing). A tithe of five gold a season from a brother; three owed and the prior speaks, four and the grey comes off. A sermon at the fair each year, yours or the rival novice's.
- **Its own people.** The prior of the house and the house's wing — every faith's doctrine names a dispute, and the house is of the open side or the strict one; the rival at the other lamp; the penitent who comes to a keeper's door; the doubter who keeps the offices with nothing behind them; the preacher at the well.
- **Its own stories.** The preacher of the other wing, to denounce, hear, or shelter under the shrine's roof. The tithe sent for in a hungry year: collect it, forgive it, or pay it yourself. The relic on your shelf that the house would like on its altar. The doubter's secret: tell the prior, keep it, or argue the light back into them — and perhaps lose your own. The court's purge at the shrine door, with the prior's wing deciding whether the door is open or shut, and you the one at it: open it, shut it, or stand in it — some keepers have died in doorways, and those are the ones with feast days. The prior's inquiry when a vow is broken: confess and do penance, deny and be believed or not, or take the grey off yourself. A confession under the lamp: counsel, absolve, or tell the court.
- **Ways out.** A shrine of your own to keep after five years, with a stipend instead of a tithe and a flock; the priory, when the old prior dies and the chapter votes; lapsing; the defrocking; the doorway.
- **Legacy.** A parent in the cloth leaves a name in the house's book, and the heir's novice's door opens on it; a defrocked parent's child starts in the Order's shade.
- Devotion and the Order's standing, which many things raised and nothing read, are read now: the Order knows a name at forty, and the house watches your devotion.

Version 0.68.0 is Battlecraft: the field you can read, the arts of a calling, what a fight costs, who stands with you, and the bestiary. New lives only.

- **The field.** Two ranks a side. Warriors and rogues stand in front, mages and healers behind; nothing without a bow, a spell or a pack reaches the back rank while the front stands, and a wolf pack flanks. Step back or step up, and it costs you the action. Portraits, bars and an intent glyph on every foe; the log folded beneath.
- **The arts.** Each calling keeps its two gifts and its two earned techniques, and beyond them a tree: two arts at the second tier, four at the third, seventy-two in all — the guardian's hook that drags a foe out of the back rank, the thief's pitch flask, the ranger's pinning shot, the bard's chorus of blades. Three days and fifteen gold with your mentor for the second tier; four days and forty, two arts of the second tier, and the mentor's own place for the third. Only the calling that owns an art can use it.
- **Foes that force tactics.** A person who has fought before takes the measure of a stroke repeated: the third identical blow is turned and answered. Cold holds a foe for the blow that shatters it; fire on oil burns twice. Charges still want interrupting, wards still want breaking, the bosses still turn.
- **Danger by place, not by level.** What you meet in a barrow is a barrow's; it does not grow because you have. The roaming fights take their tier from where you stand: a village's road is a village's, a ruin's is a ruin's.
- **What a fight costs.** Beaten, you are hurt: bruised ribs, a broken arm, cracked ribs, a gashed leg, an eye. A broken arm is six weeks and a slow hand; a gashed leg left to itself mends crooked, and a limp ends your soldiering; a wound a week without a healer goes bad and reaches the health book. Two days and eight gold with the healer set it straight in half the time.
- **Who stands with you.** Companions have loyalty now: it rises when you win together, falls when you walk away from a fight and leave them lying, falls when you do what they would not. Below twenty they say so at the fire — let them go, pay them, or promise. Down in a fight you win, they are carried out and mend for a fortnight; down in a fight you lose or leave, half the time they do not get up, and the party remembers what you did with the body. At eighty you fight as one: shield and spear, fire on the oil, the mark and the blade.
- **The bestiary.** Every creature met, fought and killed is written down. Kill a kind twice, or read it in the archive, or know natural philosophy, and you know what it fears before it shows you. The heir inherits what the parent knew.
- **Not worth the telling.** A fight your party outweighs by far can be settled in a line: a few scratches, the spoils, and on.
- Old saves keep the fight they had.

Version 0.67.0 is The delve: dungeons as places with a history. New lives only.

- **A place with a history.** Every delve has a premise — who built it, what went wrong, what lives there now, why you came — and every room serves it. The five campaign dungeons are rebuilt on it first: the Drowned Cistern and its bound keeper, the Buried Archive and the last archivist, the Moonroot Caverns and the Thornheart, the Broken Watchtower and its captain, the Covenant Core and the anchor warden. Under any town, the board's uncharted descents are a barrow, a root-hollow, a fallen tower or a deep cave, each with a reason to be feared and a rumour of how many came back.
- **Fewer rooms, each worth entering.** One template: an approach, a hub, two or three chambers, a secret the right eyes find, the lair, and the way — the objective, or the drop to the next floor. Shortcuts the builders hid take you up behind everything, so going back matters. Deeper floors: better finds, worse odds.
- **Light and supplies.** Six gold at the door for three torches, a rope, a flask of oil and a stick of chalk, if you have it; otherwise a candle-stub. A torch is four hours; a room or a search is an hour; twelve hours below is a day above. In the dark nothing is noticed, nothing can be searched, and what bites bites harder. Rope for the drop or skin; oil to fire a nest and wake the halls; chalk so the way back costs an hour and not two; a ration to camp, and camp only where you have made the room safe.
- **What you notice depends on who you are.** Letters read the lintel; Old Elvish understands the warning; a historian knows where the weakness will be; a miner hears the ceiling settle and the hollow wall; a thief sees the wire; a hunter reads the spoor and hears what is breathing behind a door; an arcanist feels the ward thin; a believer's skin prickles at a desecration; the anatomist reads the dead. A noticed trap is a trap stepped round.
- **The dungeon reacts.** Noise from fighting, searching, fire wakes the halls: what you meet is a tier harder, and past a point the lair stops waiting and comes to you. Kill the brood mother and the young scatter. Sneak past the lair to the objective and it hears you.
- **It is deadly.** What lives at the bottom is not scaled to you. Defeat below ground is death; on the gentle setting your companions carry you out and the place is left awake.
- **It touches the world above.** A cleared lair is a quieter town — the road past it walked after dark, the place remembering. A woken lair left alone grows bolder by the season until something comes up out of it and takes a child from the edge houses, and the village asks who will go down after it. What you carry up has an owner: the shrine, a guild, a family two streets over — return it, sell it to a dealer who does not ask, or keep it on the shelf where its owner may one day see it.
- **Legacy.** An heir takes up the same places: what was put to rest stays quiet, what was woken is still awake, and what sat on the shelf is theirs.
- **Also fixed.** On the road, the shrine keeper's bowl, the pilgrims' pot and the reader's cup are shut when the purse is short instead of refusing you after you chose them.
- Old saves keep their grid mazes, which are untouched.

Version 0.66.0 is The guild, the first path built at full depth. New lives only.

- **Ways in.** Born to it: one trade birth in two is a guild family, the parent works the craft, and the family's name puts yours on the rolls when you take up the trade. Apprenticed: three years at a master's bench and the journeyman's paper opens the smiths' or the miners' hall. Bought: eighty gold on the table, after the hall has seen your work, and years before it forgets what you paid. Recruited: the hall comes for a hand it saw stand with it outside the yard gate, or work it would rather have inside than against it. Inherited: a master's name in the old book, and the dues owed on it.
- **Daily life changes.** Dues every season (eight gold for a journeyman, twelve for a master, none for a warden), arrears when the purse is empty, the warden at the door at three and the name struck at four. The hall's work each spring, six days for the hall's pay and the warden's book, a fine before the table if a year passes with it undone. A journeyman serves three seasons in other regions' halls before the wardens will look at a masterpiece; fourteen days with the door shut, and then they look, and say the word or do not (sweeten the chest and everyone knows by evening; an honest nature cannot). Masters pay the court's tax, take apprentices, and hold a bench with their own mark. Under the guild's monopoly no one works the trade without its mark; members get the hall's share of the prices and the street's resentment. The old "ask the guild to recognise you" yields to the hall's own ranks.
- **Its own people.** The warden who reads your name at the table and writes in the book; the rival at the next bench and the hall's prize at the fair, yours or theirs each year; the apprentice who is yours for three years and then, by your word at the table or without it, a journeyman; the rival guild across the map.
- **Its own stories.** The contract the court gives the hall and your faith forbids: work it, refuse it at your bench and be fined and remembered, or turn the table if it can be moved. A stranger who buys you a drink and asks about the quench, the cipher, the assay: sell it for sixty (a hundred, if you are greedy), refuse him, or tell the warden; a sold secret can come back to your bench. The warden dies and the table chooses: stand against the rival, back them, or stay out of it. The apprentice's paper. The warden at the door for the dues.
- **The world reacts, and the ways out.** The court marks a bench that refused its contract; the street leaves a loaf on the step. A member who works through the hall's own strike is struck out; walk out as a journeyman and the hall shrugs, as a master and it remembers. Rise to the wardenship, betray it, be cast out, or leave.
- **Legacy.** The name on the rolls passes to an heir as a journeyman's place with the road years counted served; the arrears pass as debt; the rival at the bench who beat you at the fair carries the feud to your child; a family that made a master is a guild family after.
- **Also fixed.** A life gathered more ties (names that hate yours, names that owe you a roof) than its save would reload with; ties are capped at ten now and a creditor is never the one forgotten. An heir of a new-edition life inherits the living world's actors and news instead of a fresh world that then ticked two hundred seasons at once.

Version 0.65.2 is The living world, third slice: bonds that fade, and the road to town for the lives born outside one. New lives only.

- **Bonds fade when ignored.** A friend, a rival or a relative you have not sent word to or sat at a table with in three years loses a little each year, down to a nod in the street; any change at all, up or down, starts the clock again. The book notes it once, when a name you used to say goes.
- **The road at fourteen.** A life born at a brook, a forest, a marsh or a gate used to reach eighteen with no school and no trade, because the school and the trades are in town and the generated past never walked there. Now it does, at fourteen, resting in the open when it is tired: a shrine school late, a bench, a trade. Two in five generated lives were born outside a town.

Version 0.65.1 is The living world, second slice: people with wants of their own, the carts that follow a war and the sickness they carry, and places that change. New lives only.

- **People have wants.** Once in a while someone you know acts on one. A rival sets up across the square in your trade and charges half: match them, out-work them, show the town the shoddy work (if the town will listen), or let it choose; two seasons later the bench across the square closes or stays. A friend comes to the door after dark and asks you to say they were with you: say it (and one time in three it comes out on you both, and the second lie comes easier), refuse it and watch the friendship go out of their face, or say nothing. A parent takes to bed: at home, nurse them, pay a physician, or leave it to the others; away, drop everything and go (sixty days to reach the bedside, kept by arriving and missed by the calendar), send money, write, or finish what you are doing. A season later they sit up and ask what day it is, or they do not.
- **Carts from the border.** Refugees follow a war into the next region, bread is dearer and the byres are full, and one cart in four carries the sickness: lime on the doorsteps for three seasons, and a sickbed in your own house.
- **Places change.** The wolves, a strike, marked doors, scaffolding on the square, the carts, the sickness and the guild's mark are all written where the place view reads them, for as long as the place remembers; a monopoly held to its end raises the guild's hall on the square.
- **Still to come in the living world:** bonds that fade when ignored, the curses rebuilt on the thread engine, monster hunts that are dungeons, and the lives born outside a settlement.

Version 0.65.0 is The living world, first slice: stories happen everywhere, and reach you through your own people. New lives only.

- **Actors with goals.** The six courts, three guilds whose reach is the whole map, and two wolf packs in the hills each hold a goal and advance it every season whether or not you are looking: a court's claim on a neighbour's town, a levy, a purge of one people, a new hall; a guild's contract or its monopoly; the pack's descent, fastest in a hard winter. Goals take years; the calendar no longer starts wars on its own.
- **Collisions make events.** A finished claim is a war, fought through the regional world (the front, the deployments, the settlement). A levy is money at every hearth in the region, and what the purse lacks is written against the house. A purge marks doors for four seasons and crosses the border. A contract refused is a strike where the contract was. The wolves take something from a village in the night. Events mark the places they touch and leave.
- **Events reach you through your own people.** The levy comes for kin of levy age: pay the exemption, go in their place, or let them go and hear in a year. The strike finds your trade, your bench or your commission: down tools, work through it and be chalked, or carry terms. The wolves find your village: go up the mountain, pay hunters, or stay by the fire and they come back. The purge finds the partner, kin or child of the marked people, or you: hide them, give their name first, or send them down the road and hear by the thaw. What finds no one stays word from the road.
- **Choices write back.** Standing with the village and the court, bonds, the family's ties (a guild that hates you, a sibling who will not forgive), a nature that closes, and a memory on the thread that comes back a season or four later.
- **The world** sits on the Life page: what is within a season's walk, what came to your door, word from the road.
- **Still to come in the living world:** people with wants of their own (a rival who undercuts you, a friend who asks you to lie, a mother who needs you home three weeks away), places that change for a decade, the curses rebuilt on the same engine, more threads per event.

Version 0.64.1 is Education, second slice: above the grammar school, the capitals; beside you on the bench, two names you keep for life; and the apprenticeship as a bond rather than a free course. New lives only.

- **The institutions of the capitals.** The University, the War College, the Collegium, the Seminary and the School of Law, each in the first settlement of its kind. Each wants the grammar certificate, an entrance examination once a year from sixteen (5 gold a sitting, not returned), and 120 gold a year (the Seminary 60) unless the one free place, a high house, a classmate who rose or a family friend pays. Each can turn a birth away at the door: the War College does not commission the bound and wants gentle birth or a weapon's name; the Collegium wants arcana already and a region whose law allows the work; the Seminary devotion the keepers can see; the School of Law a tongue that has studied rhetoric. Three years, six lectures a year, a major chosen at the rolls, the examiners at the end. What they make you: a doctor of the University (the healers' door and the healers' wages), an officer (an officer's pay), a licensed mage, holy orders, a clerk of the court (who reads the lender his own paper back, and pays the lawful tenth).
- **Studies that fade.** Sixteen subjects finer than the eight skills (law, medicine, history, rhetoric, anatomy, thaumaturgy, command, liturgy and the rest), raised by electives and lectures. Three idle years and a study begins to go, down to half of what it reached; what you use, you keep. Credentials read them.
- **The bench.** Enrol at the grammar school and two classmates sit beside you, with names, natures and a subject each. Once a year a prize is contested between you (the Deceitful see a third way). They remember. At twenty-five word comes of what became of them; at forty, again: a magistrate who says your name carefully, a guild master who does not answer letters, a heretic on trial, a grave. One who rose can pay your way into an institution.
- **The bond.** The apprenticeship is three years at a master's bench: two gold a lesson, other schools and trades shut while it runs, the master's temper once a year (take it, answer back, or walk out). Buy it back for thirty gold, or break it and the master hates your family's name and the guild knows. Serve it and you are a journeyman in the craft, with a journeyman's start. In the generated past a child with no school by twelve is put to a bench, and takes up the trade.
- **Standing.** A degree is received by the academy and resented in the street: the low-born educated hear "scholar" said unkindly now and then.
- **Fixed from the first slice:** the school examinations and the archivist's lessons spent their days (and the archivist's fee) twice; and the engine's action receipt ("Sit the examination. 2 days passed.") overwrote every schooling line in the book. Both corrected.

Version 0.64.0 is Education, first slice: being lettered is rare, earned and worth something. It applies to new lives; saves from earlier versions keep their old rules.

- **Letters are earned.** A new life cannot read until someone teaches it. A high house has a tutor by eight; a literate trade house teaches at its own table by ten, most of the time; a village or shrine school certificate is letters; a grown person can learn from the archivist, four afternoons at three gold each. The unlettered cannot read on their own, and put their mark to the lender's paper at a rate he did not say aloud.
- **School has a price and a door.** The shrine teaches for nothing and the keeper takes one pupil, decided once at six (a common child two in five, a bound one one in twelve, a word from a family friend helps). The village school wants ten gold a year, paid by a wealthy house for its children and from the purse by everyone else. The grammar school expects letters, an entrance examination once a year from twelve, and forty gold a year unless you passed the scholarship examination: three places, one sitting in a life.
- **Aptitude is real.** Intellect, how you have done so far, and nature (the Curious learn faster, the Impatient struggle, Stress drags) move every lesson and every examination.
- **Electives.** The grammar years offer one elective a year from a seeded four (rhetoric, anatomy, heraldry, accounting, astronomy, theology, Old Elvish, siegecraft, natural philosophy, law), four lessons each, then the master's grade and a skill.
- **Credentials gate the learned trades.** The healers want letters and a grammar certificate or anatomy; the archive wants the certificate; a cartographer must read; a merchant must read or be born to a trade house; the alchemists want letters and natural philosophy. The smithy, the field and the road never asked.
- **The past knows it.** A generated life sits the examinations and takes its lessons, so entering at eighteen as a common child you read about half the time, as a bound child almost never, as a trade child often with a grammar certificate, as gentry always.
- **Still to come in Education:** the institutions of the capitals (university, war college, collegium, seminary, law school), the wider skill list that fades without use, classmates as people, standing for the educated, the apprenticeship bond.

Version 0.63.0 is Voice: the game reads like its art. It applies to new lives; saves from earlier versions keep their old text.

- **Live the season.** A command beside Age up: a quarter of a year that stops only for things that matter. A slight complaint is seen to at home without stopping the calendar; what is getting worse, the mind, a promise coming due and the birthday still ask. The last season of a year runs to the birthday and its page.
- **One voice.** The actions and scenes every life meets are told as situations, in the register of the engraved art, instead of as ledgers: "Work a shift — Three days of it. The pay comes after." "A fever. It is getting worse. Nobody has seen to it yet." The figures are still there, behind *Show the numbers* in the reading settings. Nothing about what a choice does has changed.
- **Right now.** The Life page opens with the three to five things that matter today — a body getting worse, money owed, the work, a child's lesson or the lack of one, a cold house — written as situations, with everything else beneath.
- **The book.** The journal reads under chapter headings by age band (The cradle, The yard, The threshold, The road, The work, The weight, The long evening), and a closed book carries an epitaph that judges the life against what it set out to do: "Kalis Meratti, of Ore Pass. The winter took them. They died owing Mavuno Navessa."
- **For developers:** `node scripts/balance/run-lives.mjs --season` lives by season and reports stops a year.

Version 0.62.0 is Birth and nature: two children born the same year in the same town now live different lives. It applies to new lives; saves from earlier versions keep their old rules.

- **Station is fate's.** You choose ancestry, looks, name, pronouns and nature. Station is rolled by rarity: bound or destitute about 35 births in a hundred, common and free 40, trade 17, gentry 6, noble under 2, royal blood under one in two hundred. Each people has its own ladder (a clanless dwarven family, an elder elven line, the Nest-mother's line).
- **A birth decides.** Whether the family is free, bound to the land or owned; what it owes and to whom; whether anyone at home reads, and so which schools would take you (the bound are not schooled unless someone at home reads; the academy does not take them); how the house eats and what it sleeps under; who the family knows, owes and hates. At eighteen the house hands over a portion, or its debt and bond. Gentry and above draw rents; the bound pay dues.
- **Childhood survival is uneven.** A thin diet and a leaking roof make every fever more likely and worse. No healer comes to a door that cannot pay; trade households pay for one and feel it; gentry keep a physician. In a poor house the dark of the year is a hard winter: from eight it is a decision (give your portion away, keep bread back, endure, or take a hare from the lord's wood if you are brave or sly), and the youngest in the house may not see the thaw. Sibling deaths and, rarely, your own are written with weight. A life that dies before the age you chose to enter it is not the one you enter; fate draws another.
- **Nature has teeth.** Traits no longer buy attribute points; a nature is a trade-off chosen for who you want to be. Some answers exist only for some natures: the Deceitful can alter the lender's ledger, the Brave can stand in the door. Life writes the rest: how you cope when you break, a grave dug too young, two winters of giving or hoarding, and a trait acted against for years flips to its opposite. People hold you by what their own people respect: an orc warms to the Honest and the Brave, a goblin to the Deceitful and the Ambitious.
- **Also:** you can pay down what you owe from the household panel (there was no way to before); the oldest a life can begin scales with its people (a goblin cannot enter at eighty).
- **For developers:** `node scripts/balance/run-lives.mjs --start 0` plays lives from birth and reports by station.

Version 0.61.0 is Pressure: the first release where a life can go wrong. It applies to new lives; saves from earlier versions keep their old rules.

- **Money is real.** Living costs went up and lodgings cost rent. What you cannot pay is owed, and owed money grows a tenth a year. At three years of costs the lender comes: sell the house, bind yourself to a year of unpaid labour, plead, or leave in the night. The parish carries a household once, in the first adult year, and never again.
- **Seasons bring luck, mostly bad.** A thin harvest, a war levy, a theft, a season with no work, a roof, a rent rise, a sick season; less often a windfall or a good year. Waiting out a year earns less than working it.
- **Mood settles.** Joy and Stress drift toward a level set by your circumstances instead of sitting at 100 and 0. Debt, illness and poverty weigh; a partner, children and comfort lift. At the top of the Stress scale something gives, and how you cope marks you.
- **People die.** From the elder age the odds climb each year, faster for a worn body or an untreated illness, which can now kill at any age. A comfortable human life ends in its seventies or eighties, not past a hundred. A shift needs some Focus left.
- **For developers:** `node scripts/balance/run-lives.mjs` plays seeded lives with simple policies and reports the measures the Design Bible targets. Measured from 18 over 90 years: median age at death 101 → 71–75, lifetime gold 60k → 10–12k, resting Joy 100 → about 55.

Version 0.60.0 is the first release of a new edition. It changes nothing about how the game plays yet; it makes the foundation safe and fast for what comes next.

- **Saves no longer have a size ceiling.** Lives are stored in the browser's IndexedDB instead of localStorage, which capped a save at about 5 MB and was reached within one long life. Existing saves are moved over automatically the first time the new version opens.
- **The engine is 6–10× faster.** Starting a life at 18 takes 8 seconds instead of 49; a year of Age up takes a quarter of a second instead of nearly three. Every generated outcome is identical to before.
- **Fixes:** history entries no longer turn into "This marks an earlier event" placeholders; worlds no longer contain two places with the same name; "a alchemist" and lowercase weather sentences; one corner notice at a time instead of three stacked; phone navigation labels fit on one line.
- **For developers:** `npm run test:fast` (minutes) and `npm run test:slow` (the long cohorts) replace one multi-hour run; `npm run bench` times the engine; tests that need the unshipped `reference-art/` folder skip themselves. See `CLAUDE.md` for the working rules and `docs/design-direction` in the project notes for where the game is going.

## Run locally

Requires Node.js 18 or newer for tests and Python 3 for the local web server. There are no package dependencies, required build step, or external AI services.

```sh
npm start
```

Open [http://localhost:3000](http://localhost:3000). Keep the server running while playing; stop it with Ctrl+C. The source `dist/index.html` needs HTTP because it uses JavaScript modules.

```sh
npm test
```

For the current serial release gate, run `npm run release:check`. It includes every discovered regression test and offline checker, exact source/version/bundle checksums, and separate honest browser/screen-reader statuses. See [the current release contract](docs/current-release-gate.md) for incomplete modes, retained historical compatibility and known blocking test debt. `npm test` remains the raw historical aggregate, not a release certification. Explicit core-only diagnostics and exact-source pause/resume are documented there; omitted stress coverage remains NOT RUN, not a full pass.

The tests use Node's built-in test runner. They cover connected life journeys, family and school, age gates, seeded geography and lore, earned callings, conversations, campaign outcomes, dungeons, transformations, building projects, equipment, combat, professions and wages, health and travel, inheritance across generations, and compatibility with genuine earlier saves. Some older tests still assume the retired remote-service menus, planning offers or original literal prose; the v0.28.0 acceptance command and its actual results are recorded in [the implementation record](docs/world-expansion-focus-14.md).

## Save your life

The game automatically saves after each action, choice, reply, and combat turn in this browser's local storage. Use **Continue** or **Load life** on the title screen to resume. Each of the three slots keeps a complete independent world; creating a replacement asks first and does not replace a life until character creation finishes.

Use **Export** to download a JSON backup and **Import save** to restore one, including an unfinished event, conversation, battle, dungeon, or building project. Earlier prototype saves migrate automatically, including paid work and unfinished journeys; their history and pending decisions remain intact. Old family records reference the existing caregiver without inventing parents. An unfinished v0.2 annual plan has a compatibility control to complete it. Invalid files are rejected without replacing the current life. Browser storage is tied to the browser profile and site address; use Export/Import to move a life between the hosted and offline versions, or keep an export before clearing site data or moving to another device.

## Playing a life

Choose a starting age from zero to eighty and an origin among your ancestry’s beginnings, or leave it to fate. Ancestry, identity, heritage, a whole portrait and starting age can be chosen; you may edit the name, fill up to three compatible nature slots and assign an age-based allowance across Strength, Agility, Vitality, Intellect, Willpower and Presence. Birth has no points; the attribute allowance grows by one per three starting years, up to eighteen total, with six per attribute. Birthplace, family, wealth, beliefs and tastes come from the seed. **Cast the bones** draws fresh circumstances and a face while respecting your chosen ancestry, identity, origin, heritage and age. **Choose portrait** opens the book of illustrations for your current age. Presentation and collection filters help find visible faces, cloaks, masks and other painted looks. **Show heritage locks** lets you inspect the rest of the age collection; unavailable portraits explain their heritage requirement. Human and Elf selections use the actual illustrated complexion, and Beastfolk lineages follow the illustrated animal family. Other homelands supply lore without restricting a calling or costume.

During play, open **Character → Identity → Choose portrait** to change your face for the current life stage. Infant portraits are available at 0–2, children at 3–9, adolescents at 10–17, adults at 18–39, mature faces at 40–54, older faces at 55–64 and elders from 65. Each stage remembers its choice through saves and reloads. Age changes use a matching illustration until you choose one for that stage. Presentation does not change pronouns; armor or masks pictured in a portrait do not grant equipment. Finish a pending battle before changing its recorded face. During an active transformation, **Portrait status** explains that hunger, humanity or the beast’s form determines the curse painting; the saved mortal face remains intact. An older save can declare heritage once; new lives record it at creation.

Starting later generates your earlier years and leaves that birthday for your first decision. You have no assigned combat class. **Age up** advances toward the next birthday and pauses for meaningful decisions, commitments, and supernatural needs. Early decisions belong to your guardians; school and training become available as you grow. Travel opens at fourteen, professions and dungeons at sixteen, and advanced callings and courtship at eighteen. Childhood choices develop the actual skills used by adult callings.

Your family record keeps present parents, siblings, caregivers and absent family. The origin and seed draw your initial household. An orphaned or homeless child has an actual shelter guardian and free support routes. Find the relevant teacher in Explore: community and shrine schools open at six, academy study and apprenticeships at twelve. Attend lessons, answer school dilemmas and prepare for examinations to earn qualifications. Free scholarships and supervised service keep education accessible without family money.

Use **Relationships** to review people you have met, relationship status and shared memories. Approach a present person in **Explore** for portrait dialogue; explicit replies affect what that person knows and remembers. **Explore** shows the seeded region, routes, and your moving character piece. **Journal** keeps chosen objectives and outcomes visible. Your portrait opens character customization and earned callings. Inventory holds your equipped portrait and carried items, with search, sorting and equipment changes. Buy or sell at an actual market. Gather in the field and visit a local craftsperson to make equipment. Action rows describe requirements, prices, and time costs before you act.

From sixteen, visit a settlement's work steward to choose a profession, work a shift or practice. Ask “Any Commissions Available?” to hear eligible offers before accepting; your current commission stays visible when you return. Named customers specify materials, quality, deadlines and payment. A separate named Materials Merchant at the Market sells ore, cloth, wood, stone, hide and crystal in the existing three-unit, one-day bundles. A local craftsperson offers equipment work and earned professional projects. Meet guild mentors and faction representatives in their actual areas; accepted tasks remain in Journal. Activities shows personal pursuits and concise controls for existing unfinished work.

## RPG scope

Fresh worlds connect thirteen core sites and two to six optional sites through seeded roads; twelve sites offer starting birthplaces. Earlier maps retain their original geography on import. Twelve earned callings span martial, magical, and stealth roles, with mentor and skill requirements. Three companions support an active party of up to four, with turn order, abilities, defense, items, retreat, and nonlethal defeat.

Twenty main quests and returning sidequests follow childhood revelations into investigations, allies, five branching dungeons, bosses, a settlement crisis, and six distinct endings. Changed settlements retain their place on the map and alter available services. Civilian systems include twelve professions, commissioned goods, staffed shops, lifetime works, wellbeing and care, housing, family, education, courtship, marriage, recorded children and successor play, personal goals, six building types, and twenty-eight equipment pieces. Eight ancestries have complete illustrated portraits with age and heritage restrictions. Hair, beards, headwear and clothing belong to each painting; no layered hair or face overlays are rendered. Preferences, temperament, beliefs, upbringing, difficulty, and the world seed support different lives.

Vampirism can begin as a disease after a suffered bite in darkness or a dungeon, or through the twilight patron’s invitation. Four original blood lineages carry different gifts and lore. Hunger changes social presence, stress, humanity and the illustrated face; starvation and sunlight can kill. Consented sessions and animal sustenance remain available. A well-fed vampire avoids ordinary old-age mortality. Lycanthropy brings an untrained beast, a visible 28-day lunar cycle, costly containment, preparation and gradual mastery over real moons. Witnesses and regional hunter orders can respond to actual local exposure; a capital court can end this life. Both have costly cure routes. Only one major transformation can be active at a time. Both remain connected to existing professions, companions, and local consequences.

This is a single-player RPG with six law and faith regions within one seeded world, authored situations, destination travel, settlement interiors and layered civic simulation. It does not offer multiplayer or direct kingdom management. The latest expansion requirements are in [DYNASTY_CONTRACT.md](DYNASTY_CONTRACT.md).

Sound is optional and off by default. Enable it in Settings; the original score follows the actual region, travel, battle and curse scene after a gesture. Audio preferences are separate from your saved life, and restoring them never starts playback. All simulation and combat run locally in the browser. The files in `dist/` can be served by any static HTTP host.
