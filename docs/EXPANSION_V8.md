# Hearth & Oath v0.8: lives that keep developing

This expansion follows the [v0.7 playtest audit](playtest/REPORT.md). Its main additions give a lifelong civilian a future worth building, give people reasons to return after their first meeting, and make expeditions depend on the conduct and relationships developed above ground.

The six expansion routes have been played through **28 distinct complete lives and 5,242 public game transitions**. Every original character begins at age zero and reaches actual retirement. Continued children are part of their family's route, rather than additional complete lives. Reruns and failed driver attempts are not counted as new characters.

## People have work and futures beyond yours

An accepted commission can begin a relationship with a recurring household. The household remembers the delivered work, its price and what you honestly explained. Its later concern can involve the actual object or service: a disputed song, a shifting crossing, a cloudy preparation, a split mine support or a care plan that needs another hearing.

Helping requires actual inspection, repair, materials or teaching. You can also decline honestly, or return to a deferred plan after earning money. A discussion alone produces neither wages nor completed work.

Your actual staff can ask for signed credit, safer hours and an independent beginning. A qualified apprentice's departure ends their paid place in your roster while retaining their own correspondence and goal. Non-family peers can choose occupations, relocate, retire and die. Later adult neighbors enter a long-lived character's social circle; a replacement household has a new identity instead of reviving a dead customer.

These developments support civilian roleplay. Staying a blacksmith, healer, merchant or scholar can produce lasting relationships and disagreements without making an adventure or combat class mandatory.

## Savings can become a lasting public ambition

Five undertakings give an established civilian meaningful goals: a learning hall, clinic, wayhouse, common garden or memorial refuge. Each has a chosen charter, three separately agreed construction milestones, an actual location, named beneficiaries and a protected operating reserve.

Completing one requires **37–50 actual days, 3,000–5,500 gold and gathered materials**. Its endowment funds approximately ten operating years. Further upkeep is an explicit choice; the institution does not secretly withdraw household money or pay its reserve back into the player's purse.

The buildings provide specific services while they are funded and operating:

| Undertaking | Connection to ordinary life |
| --- | --- |
| Learning hall | Affordable local schooling or supported practical learning; a building does not grant a qualification. |
| Clinic | Affordable attended care and a longer actual recovery plan. |
| Wayhouse | Lower danger on a newly quoted relevant road journey. |
| Common garden | Faster recovery of real gathering stocks, or basic winter food support. |
| Memorial refuge | A surviving place for displaced households and household lunar planning, while retaining the lost town's history. |

Beneficiaries return with their own accounts. You can listen, fund a practical response or delegate it. Actual children can learn through supported work. A successor inherits the same charter, people and protected reserve and can choose stewardship; the money is not duplicated as a cash inheritance.

## Learning and family decisions have personal consequences

Four school routes now offer eight distinct supervised projects. Real attended sessions unlock work about oral history, safe crossings, healing gardens, ancestral consent, wards, restricted records, repairs and fair measures. Completing the project leads to a decision and actual learning. From twelve, a pupil can discuss inquiry, service or practical learning without receiving a free skill or a predetermined class.

Teachers retire and hand their school to a named successor. Their old identity and correspondence remain. Pending examinations and conversations finish before a handoff, including older saved decisions.

A values discussion reveals a person's own tastes and convictions. Relationship views show what you actually know, including shared preferences and differences. Respectful disagreement and dismissing someone's wishes leave different memories; a preference is not a hidden attractiveness score or a compulsory romance gate.

Older adoption introduces a young person around six, twelve or sixteen who already has a name, appearance, birthday, guardian, preferences and learning. Meeting, listening and preparing a home take real days. Adoption requires their agreement. A refusal can lead to respectful learning support while they remain in their own home. A partner is not silently assigned co-parenthood.

Children's school projects develop the child's skills and traits, rather than copying a reward onto the parent. Continuing as a child preserves that person's original identity and learning in the same dated world.

## An earned calling has a longer story

All twelve callings have an optional formation after their original initiation. The guardian must consider who an orderly shelter excludes; the knight questions a sealed convoy's priorities; the healer responds to a patient's refusal; the necromancer honors a witness's limits; the assassin checks whose interests a paid dossier conceals. Each calling has its own problem, two different practices and a named technique.

Formation requires **six days of inquiry, eight days of chosen practice and two days of mentor review**. You can promise community service or agree to an independent practice, then seek public recognition using permitted sources or keep a private signed account. The original class qualification remains intact. Completion records a technique, standing and mentor trust, without stacking another attribute or combat bonus.

The three companions have eight-day requests about their own goals: a protector negotiates whose safety an order serves, a healer makes room for rest and shared care, and a scout checks a sibling's route without demanding a reunion. Their memories, campaign experience and privately disclosed knowledge influence the discussion. An honest refusal is permitted, and helping with one request does not erase an existing boundary.

## Dungeons remember a different expedition

The five critical expedition sites now receive saved, seeded definitions with three layouts, three authored concerns and three inhabitant motives per site. Each has eight connected rooms, including two selected optional chambers, with named custodians and witnesses. The mandatory campaign discoveries remain reachable.

Optional routes let you protect a refuge, repair neglected access, preserve an account or recover a useful tool while leaving its evidence behind. You can provide practical aid without copying someone's private records. Listening alone grants no room clear or combat reward.

Negotiation depends on actual training, aid, evidence and a trusted living companion's relevant memory. A completed calling formation can provide an earned account in a matching site. This connection was played directly: a Ranger completed the sixteen-day formation, repaired a Moonroot service route without copying its records, then used the formation as the missing proof for a negotiated guardian passage. The game still required aid, listening and trained judgment.

The final discovery can be kept as a private copy, published with a witness's actual stated limits or accompanied by a material repair. The result stays in the expedition record and survives succession. Repairing one channel does not decide the whole campaign or resurrect a destroyed town.

## Failure and supernatural life have ongoing agreements

A witnessed local incident can become a particular hearing with its actual allegation, evidence and place. The player can arrange paid restitution, free community service, a willing named advocate or an escape with a visible chance. Failed flight leads to a playable detention and supervised restitution route. It does not automatically end the character's life or erase their skills. Secret studies and undisclosed conditions create no invented accusation.

Vampire donors are actual living adult contacts with their own seeded willingness. Agreeing requires informed discussion and a real day; it grants no feeding relief. Attended sessions use real time, an agreed fee and a fourteen-day interval. Refusal, privacy and animal sustenance remain valid choices. Absence, death or withdrawn willingness prevents participation.

Moon-watch circles and household plans name the people, place and particular coming full moon. Preparation must actually finish before that moon. Children have separate safe space; nobody becomes a participant or learns a secret without an agreement. Preparation rewards cannot be multiplied by recreating plans for the same moon.

These arrangements connect with civilian institutions: a funded clinic supports attended recovery, a road project affects new travel quotations, a garden changes stock recovery only during its funded periods, and a refuge can provide real space for a lunar household plan.

## A saved life remains the same life

The save revision remains five. New systems start prospectively, preserving old names, resources, children, earned classes and original pending choice effects. An already entered or recorded legacy dungeon retains its original graph, choices, battle context and claimed rewards. New definitions are captured in the save rather than rerolled when a screen opens.

Committed work keeps its quoted fees, materials and actual completed days through birthdays, illness, hunger, deadlines, save/load and continuation. An interruption does not buy a completed project. Fees are not charged again when the same task resumes, and zero attendance cannot grant its finished result. Leaving work unfinished preserves its history without inventing a refund, output or completed agreement.

Succession keeps public infrastructure, world people, teacher history, property, expedition records and actual claimed caches. A child's own relationships, calling formations, donor agreements and private preparations develop through their new life. Earlier completed practices and friendships remain readable family history rather than automatic personal powers.

## Assessment and remaining depth

The game now connects more of an ordinary life to the world it changes. Professional work can create a recurring person; that person can become an institution's beneficiary; a child can learn there and inherit its stewardship; a companion's remembered experience or an earned discipline can alter a later expedition. Consequences arise from particular records and actual time instead of a universal morality number.

It remains a growing prototype with finite authored content. The new civilian system does not simulate every NPC's private finances and family. Formation currently uses a shared three-stage structure with twelve authored problems. The five critical dungeons vary routes, concerns and resolutions around fixed discoveries and enemy families. Older adoption guardians and many expedition witnesses are named people with bounded roles, rather than unrestricted autonomous characters. The completed routes do not exhaust every seed, branch combination or household arrangement.

Further depth should follow those particular lives: an NPC's ambition returning through later circumstances, different intermediate mentor assignments, new enemy families and repeatable expeditions, and more long-term choices about who governs inherited institutions. The useful standard remains a coherent life that remembers what happened, rather than a larger menu or a claim of endless stories.
