# Portrait artwork for v0.13

Generated with the built-in ImageGen tool. The adult kin atlas was used as a style and registration reference. These are new assets; the adult plates remain available.

The source PNGs were converted to WebP without changing the art. A composition montage checked sprite registration with existing hair layers. Wolf muzzles receive a runtime hair cutout so overlapping hair cannot erase the face. This montage is an integration check, not a browser screenshot.

## infants

```text
Use case: stylized-concept.
Asset type: final production portrait sprite atlas for Hearth & Oath, a gritty medieval fantasy life simulator.
Input image 1 is a STYLE AND REGISTRATION reference, not an edit target. Create a new youth atlas matching its hand-engraved dark-fantasy etching. Natural anatomy, dense fine cross-hatching, aged bone ink on neutral near-black background, muted monochrome parchment. Absolutely no cartoons, cute chibi, anime, green color wash, 3D rendering, gradients or plastic skin.
Composition: exactly 16 square cells in a uniform edge-to-edge 4-column by 4-row grid, no gutters, borders, labels, typography or decorative elements. One head and small plain dark cloth collar in each cell. All faces are in the same three-quarter view facing LEFT, head tilted naturally; crown around y=4%, visible eye x=43% y=35%, nose tip x=22% y=47%, mouth x=30% y=60%, visible ear x=76% y=44% in each square. Every head entirely inside its cell. Bald human/elf/dwarf/halfling/orc/goblin/tiefling heads so hair layers can be added at runtime; short natural fur on wolf beastfolk only. No beards, moustaches, scars, wounds, tattoos, jewelry, helmets, masks, accessories or equipment. The portrait must clearly depict a living young person, not a weathered adult or undead creature.
Cell identities, strict row-major order:
Row 1: human boy; human girl; elf boy with pointed ears; elf girl with pointed ears.
Row 2: dwarf boy with broad rounded facial structure; dwarf girl; halfling boy with round cheeks and small ears; halfling girl.
Row 3: orc boy with broad nose and small age-appropriate lower tusks; orc girl; goblin boy with longer ears and slender face; goblin girl.
Row 4: tiefling boy with very small smooth paired horns on the forehead; tiefling girl; wolf beastfolk boy with young canine muzzle and fur ears; wolf beastfolk girl.
Each adjacent pair is unmistakably the same ancestry, differing subtly in facial shape rather than cosmetics. Keep horns/ears within the cell. All share coherent leftward pose and plain black collar.
Age-specific requirement: All 16 subjects are infants, about 1–2 years old. Rounded infant cheeks, larger forehead, small nose, tender open curious eyes, very small jaw, no wrinkles, no adult masculinity. Tiny undeveloped tusks/horns; wolf cub faces. Show head-and-shoulders infants upright with a simple swaddling collar. Youth anatomy is essential.
```
