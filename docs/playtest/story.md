# Story, world, and roleplay audit

Eight independent lives began at age zero and reached the original character’s real retirement at age 30. They used only public engine operations: age up, actual work and mentor lessons, paid equipment, real travel, scene choices, individual battle moves, and retirement. Every transition checked save validity and input immutability. No day, resource, attribute, class, or victory was granted by the harness.

The v0.6 baseline contains **1,962 public transitions and 45 won battles**. These eight identities contribute eight lives to the combined audit. Replaying their seeds and regression fixtures does not increase that count. Full scene text, offered choices, selected choices, time and money changes, journal entries, and battle moves are preserved under `artifacts/playtest/story/`; [story.json](story.json) is the compact baseline record. The original baseline summary’s first entry describes the actual successor at age 12; the compact record correctly identifies the parent’s age-30 closure separately.

## What the baseline established

The campaign is playable through all six endings without selecting a class at birth. Its destroyed places affect services and its witnessed coercion affects an actual oath and relationships. Civilian refusal works, reconsideration works, and an actual child inherits a changed world without inheriting the parent’s sacred service or pretending to have lived the parent’s prophecy.

The eight policies selected 180 distinct scene-and-choice pairs and encountered 125 distinct scene titles. This includes ordinary years, health and family interruptions, roads, dungeon rooms, and campaign scenes; it is not a claim of 125 separate campaign plots. Seven adventurers still shared the same twenty-stage Ashfall investigation. The world varies substantially in geography and people, while the main mystery retains one authored skeleton.

One initial, unfinished run ignored every health concern and reached bodily health 2 after the consent campaign. Adoption was correctly blocked. It is retained as an observed failure, not an additional completed life. Completed policies used real free care and rest when illness arose. Care needs to remain legible when a player spends an entire childhood through annual decisions.

## Changes implemented from this evidence

- **A livelihood matters to the final preparation.** Each of the twelve actual professions has a different useful contribution. Fourteen recorded work days, a relevant skill of at least two, and consumed materials are required. Smiths and miners protect structures; soldiers and cartographers prepare evacuation; healers, gatherers, alchemists, merchants, growers, and hosts supply refuge; scholars and bards support a public coalition. A preparation supplies one concrete benefit. Wealth and an unrelated combat class cannot substitute for work experience.
- **The civic year has actual work.** After an ending, a practiced professional can contribute seven real days, 12 gold, and profession-specific materials to a surviving community. Seven unpaid work days enter the career ledger; a named world account records the contribution. It is limited to once per lived year. Closed services and permanent losses remain closed and lost.
- **Aftermath has specific stories.** Eighteen ending-specific scenes rotate across successive years, with actual weather, local custom, professional experience, and household context. The chosen issue and its sources survive in the journal. Listening targets a living person. Offering help does not pretend that a funded reconstruction project has already occurred.
- **Returning to ruins has a purpose.** A one-day memorial visit records an actual surviving account at a destroyed or evacuated place. It distinguishes a lost building, a departed household, a confirmed death, and uncertainty. It cannot reopen the town or invent casualties.
- **Family relocation uses the actual birthplace.** Coast, tower, sanctuary, and fortress households no longer default to the origin category’s city or village. Families at the destroyed citadel relocate as well. The old city/village fallback remains for older saves without a recorded birthplace.
- **A keeper cannot be bound by a missed reminder.** Withdrawal is available throughout active service. A recorded term ends without automatic renewal; another year requires explicit agreement. The ending remains family history after personal service ends.
- **Preparation preserves political and practical distinctions.** Choosing reinforcement no longer conjures a representative council. Willing compacts retain the difference between a coalition and institutional negotiation. A haven reached without prepared lodgings and stores retains its shortage rather than receiving the same thriving outcome.
- **Lore recognizes a resolved story.** The lore page stops presenting a completed omen as an unanswered mystery. Inherited history remains distinct from an heir’s own experiences.

Existing saved map geometry, names, discovered lore records, pending scenes, and ending records are retained. New narrative context is generated only when a new scene opens.

## Verification and remaining expansion opportunities

The same eight identities were replayed after implementation. The expanded policies deliberately used earned profession preparation, actual material consumption, funded seven-day civic work, annual limits, ruined-place visits, keeper withdrawal, and the real heir. Separate regressions cover smith-made reinforcement through a shattering ending, actual fortress displacement, distinct aftermath years, expiry of an old missed keeper reminder, and prepared versus unprepared refuge. Those fixtures are tests, not extra lives.

The game supports a credible short life story, but it still benefits from a larger authored story library. The strongest next additions are two or three alternate regional mysteries; recurring NPCs with their own changing goals; lasting local relationships with commission customers and reconstruction workers; travel companions who disagree over a family or professional commitment; and more practical limits on how much one household can promise. Discovery should explain why a place matters before another reward button appears.

Reduce repeated statements that every decision is consensual and every record is accurate when the scene already demonstrates it. Preserve clear consequences, while giving antagonists understandable personal stakes and letting lawful choices still conflict over family, livelihood, winter stores, and public duty. Avoid multiplying mechanically identical classes, mandatory fights, unrelated annual dilemmas, and inflated claims of unlimited authored stories. A small number of events that remember concrete people and work will improve roleplay more than another disconnected menu.

This audit covers all six endings, all five authored dungeons, ten sidequests, refusal, reconsideration, and one authentic heir. It does not enumerate every combination of decision order, class, supernatural condition, dungeon fork, relationship, or procedural seed; those combinations cannot honestly be exhausted in eight or thirty lives.
